Redesign · ozterisk — identity spread, mobile rack, five phases

oz✳terisk

Ten sockets, fixed for the run. Spending a tile leaves a scar; a reward fills one back. The rack is the pressure gauge — it breathes cinnabar from eight tiles up, and overflow is the only moment the game asks you to destroy something.

11

The last two choices, and three specified outright

Marking a tile for discard and the submit button are the only remaining pieces with real interaction state. The other three are small enough that options would be noise, so they are written as spec.
11A

Marking a tile to tip out

Overflow asks you to destroy something you own. The marked state has to be unmistakable and instantly reversible.
11aLift out — the tile rises from its socket and tilts to the rail
tip out 112345678905

Uses the locked 8a language: a tile out of true is a tile in trouble. Reads at a glance and survives being small on mobile.

11bCinnabar band — a red bar strikes across the face
tip out 112345678905

Most literal: struck through means gone. But it defaces the tile, which fights the "these are objects you own" premise.

11cGhost out — the tile dims to a dashed outline of its socket
tip out 112345678905

Previews the emptiness you are about to create, which is the actual consequence. Subtlest of the three; risky as the only signal.

11B

Submit — enabling and pressing

Disabled until every slot is filled, so the enable moment is a real event. Fires once per round.
11dRise to ready — it lifts into place, then depresses on press
42Submit

One motion covers both states: the button gains height when it becomes live, loses it when pressed. Cheapest and clearest.

11eInk fill — the outline floods with cinnabar left to right
42Submit

Feels like committing ink to paper, and pairs with the chop-seal vocabulary. The fill takes ~180ms, which delays a keyboard player.

11fTile press — the button behaves exactly like a ceramic tile
42Submit

Strongest world consistency: the same 5px drop and collapse every tile has. Risk: it stops reading as a button.

11C

Specified, no options needed

Capacity meter

The ten pips never animate as a group. A single pip flicks — 90ms, gold to bone on spend, bone to gold on fire — so the eye is drawn to the one that changed, not the row.

Share confirmation

Reuse the cinnabar chop from the rejected 2b: pressing Copy stamps a small seal beside the button for 900ms, then it fades. No toast, no layout shift.

Title entrance

The ✳ tile drops in first and settles (the locked 2a bloom curve), then the wordmark fades up beneath it over 240ms. Once per session, skipped on restart.

Recommendation: 11a for marking — it borrows the locked overflow language instead of inventing a new one — and 11d for submit, keeping 11f's ceramic press feel only for the tile buttons themselves.

8

The overflow moment — four ways an 11th tile refuses to fit

The rack is full and one more tile has arrived. This animation has to make "there is no socket for this" legible in under a second, before the player is asked to choose what to tip out. Each panel loops the arrival.
8alocked · arrivalRim reject — it lands on the rail and won't sit flat
7

Cheapest to read: the tile is physically wrong — perched on the rail at an angle no seated tile ever holds. Nothing else in the rack moves, so the fault is clearly the newcomer.

8bCrowd shove — the rack tries to make room and fails
7

The whole rack participates: neighbours shove outward, the rim flares cinnabar, and one tile is squeezed up out of its socket. Best sense of crowding — but it implies the ejected tile is the one you must lose, which is not the rule.

8clocked · discardTip out — the rack itself leans and something starts to slide
7

Gravity does the explaining, and it sets up the discard gesture — you are about to tip a tile off the end. Risk: a leaning rack is a big motion to fire every few rounds.

8dHeld above — it never lands, and casts a shadow on the socket it wants
7

Quietest and the most accurate to the rule: the tile is in limbo, waiting for you to free a socket. The shadow is the only signal, which may be too subtle on a phone.

Recommendation: 8a for the arrival, then 8c's slide reused as the discard confirm — the tile you mark tips off the rail and out of frame. 8b's shove is the most satisfying but teaches the wrong rule.

7

Streak ladder — 3, 5, 8, each louder than the last

2a's bloom is the floor and fires on every correct answer. Crossing a threshold adds one layer on top and never removes one, so the ceiling is reached by accumulation rather than by swapping effects.
7alocked · tier 1Streak 3 — one jade ring
7 × 6 = 4 2

Baseline bloom plus a single ring. Reads as acknowledgement, not celebration — it has to stay quiet because it will fire constantly.

7blocked · tier 2Streak 5 — second ring, gold rim, three chips
7 × 6 = 4 2

The gold rim is the same treatment reward tiles already wear, so it reads as "this answer paid" rather than as a new visual language.

7clocked · tier 3Streak 8 — third ring, six chips
7 × 6 = 4 2

Third ring, six chips, and the brightest rim on the answer tiles. The felt stays still — the ceiling is the tiles behaving harder, not the table lighting up. Nothing above 8 escalates further.

Rule
Tier fires on the answer that crosses the threshold and on every answer after it, until the streak breaks.
Break
A wrong answer drops straight to zero — no step-down through the tiers. The loss should feel like a cliff.
Duration
Every tier resolves inside the same ~700ms window — louder, never slower, so a hot run never feels gated by animation.

ozterisk — one name, six stylistic worlds

Name locked. Each world is a full palette + type pairing, and each has to carry the wildcard family — the asterisk is the brand mark and a future tile face, so every style shows ✳ all · 1–5 · even as tiles.
5aPhosphor terminal

* 1-5 EVEN

Wildcards are trivially expressible as monospace labels — `1-5`, `EVEN`, `ODD` all fit the face with no new drawing. Strongest system fit of the six.

Cost: abandons felt/ceramic entirely. Cold, code-literate, adult.

Warm paper, cinnabar seal, high-contrast didone. Ledger-like: the run is a record being marked, which suits permanent spending.

Brand identity — six names, six stylistic worlds

Each is a name plus the type and mark that name implies. Only 4c keeps the CJK-adjacent register; the rest drop it entirely. Icons at 72px, all legible down to 32.

* ozterisk

0–9 and the operator, said as one word. Green-on-black terminal, tight grotesk, the asterisk doing all the work as a mark.

Keeps the internal name. Reads made-for-programmers — charming to some, opaque to a parent in an app store.

4bNumeric brutalist
10 10

No word at all. The two boundary digits and a cinnabar rule — the whole game's range as the logo, and a mark that survives being 16px on a browser tab.

Unsearchable and unspeakable ("one dash zero"?). Strongest as a mark, weakest as a name.

4cEngraved house — current world, no CJK
X Tenhold ten tiles, no more

Names the constraint instead of the theme. Didone serif on a bone tile, gold hairline — the felt-and-lacquer world intact with the character removed.

Closest to what's already built, so nothing in the system gets thrown away. Slightly boutique-hotel.

4dSchoolroom formal
Nine × Ninethe table, endlessly

Says exactly what the game is drawn from. The mark is the times table's diagonal — a grid any teacher recognises, in cinnabar rather than gold.

Best for discovery and for parents. Sets a homework expectation the tile-spending tension then has to fight.

4eKiln & ash
Kilnfire ten, spend ten

Ceramics, not gambling. Tiles are fired and consumed; the palette leaves green felt for ember and ash, and every burn mark in the system becomes canon rather than decoration.

The one direction that would recolour the whole system. Also a crowded app-store word.

4fVerb, stark
Spendevery answer costs

Names the mechanic no other math game has. The mark is a single tile leaving an empty socket — the loss, not the win.

Sharpest positioning of the six, and honest about the tension. Reads finance-app cold until the tile is in frame.
