Research · M7 face-set tiles 25 Sep 2026

The roulette table already solved this

Low, High, Odd, Even and Neighbours are not new sets. They are roulette's outside bets and its oldest call bet, and players have read them at a glance for two centuries. What is new is making them tiles you spend.

Recommendation, up front

  1. Face tiles arrive on 8% of reward draws, split by the inverse of set size. Simulated at the shipped cadence, that lands Classic at 62% wins for a strong player and 50% for a typical one, both inside the band. Every face draw carries the same expected coverage; the wildcard is 0.65% of draws.
  2. Even includes 0. 2·4·6·8 is mathematically wrong in an arithmetic game, and it is the one set a player will test first: every product ending in 0.
  3. Two notation grammars, not five. Ranges (Low, High, Neighbours) engrave as a–b; parities (Odd, Even) engrave as a set. Wildcard keeps ✳. Neighbours therefore reads 4–6, provisionally.
  4. No picker stays locked. The research supports it more strongly than the decision assumed.

01 · Wildcard precedents

Every tile game with a wild piece pairs its power with a cost or a restriction. None hands it out free and unconstrained.

Scrabble · 2 of 100 · 2.0% Wild, but worth zero The blank fits anywhere and scores nothing. Flexibility is paid for in points.
Rummikub · 2 of 106 · 1.9% Wild, but dangerous to hold A joker left on the rack at round end is the heaviest penalty in the game. Hoarding is punished.
American mahjong · 8 of 152 · 5.3% Wild, but only in groups Jokers cannot stand in for a single or a pair. The closest precedent to ours: a wild piece whose power is shaped by where it is placed, and our own table's lineage.
Uno · 8 of 108 · 7.4% Wild, with a declaration Playing a wild requires naming a colour — a picker. It works because Uno has a turn pause to hold it. Ozterisk has none mid-answer.

Finding. Ozterisk's restriction is already built in: a face-set tile is only as wild as its set, and misplacing it costs a wrong answer. That is the mahjong pattern, not the Uno one. The picker would have added Uno's declaration without Uno's pause.

02 · Range and set pieces

Partial wilds — pieces that count as some values but not all — are rarer than full wilds, and the successful ones share one trait: the set is legible without reading a rule.

Roulette · outside and call bets Our five sets, already named Low / High (manque / passe), Odd / Even (impair / pair) and neighbour bets — a number plus those beside it. Zero belongs to none of the outside bets; that exclusion is the house edge, and it is deliberate.
Balatro · Wild card, Smeared Joker Membership, not substitution A Wild card counts as every suit; Smeared Joker merges suits into two colour groups. Neither asks the player to choose — the game checks membership at scoring. The same model as ours.
Dominoes · pips Quantity as pattern Pips are read as shapes, not counted. Useful for parity (a pair of dots vs. a single), weak for ranges — pips cannot say "4 to 6".

Finding — zero. Roulette excludes zero on purpose, and so, by accident, do we: Even is drafted as 2·4·6·8 while Low is 0–4. Zero cannot be in one set and missing from its parity. In a casino the exclusion is the house edge; in an arithmetic game it reads as a bug, and it will be found fast, because 0 is the most common final digit of any product with a factor of 10 or of 5 × even. Even should be 0·2·4·6·8, making Odd and Even both five.

Finding — notation. Low, High and Neighbours are all "digits from a to b". Giving them one grammar, the en dash, means a player learns it once. 5±1 introduces a second operator whose everyday meaning is tolerance or error — the wrong connotation for a tile you own outright. The cost of 4–6 is real: at a glance it shares a shape with 0–4. That is the question the tile directions must answer, not this document.

03 · Choice overload

The famous result — Iyengar and Lepper's jam display, where 24 flavours drew more browsers and fewer buyers than 6 — did not survive replication cleanly: Scheibehenne, Greifeneder and Todd's 2010 meta-analysis of 50 studies found a mean effect near zero, with overload appearing only under specific conditions. The conditions that do produce it are time pressure, options that are hard to compare, and no prior preference.

A mid-answer digit picker would have hit all three. Hick–Hyman gives decision time growing with log₂(n + 1): a ten-way picker costs roughly 3.5 bits on every face-set placement, in the most frequent action in the game. Removing it moves the choice off the clock and onto the rack, where the player studies before committing.

Finding. The remaining load is reading, not choosing: a Classic tray of 20 with six face kinds and ten digits is sixteen distinct faces. Working memory holds about four chunks (Cowan, 2001). Two notation grammars plus ✳ give three families to recognise, which fits; five bespoke faces would not.

04 · Rarity

Corrected 25 Sep. The first version of this section read Endless's 20% "kind draw" as a face-tile rate. It is not: it is the equation generator retrying one draw in five for a product the rack can spell. The face-tile rate is a new, separate dial — the share of reward tiles that come out as faces — and it had no value until the simulator below set one.

Within that rate, weight each kind by 1 / set size, so every face draw carries the same expected coverage. At the recommended 8%:

Finding — the dial is steep. Every 5 points of face rate buys roughly 9 points of win rate. 20% is past the band's top; 8% centres both players in it and lifts the typical player from a losing 36% to an even 50%.

Finding — weighting is feel, not balance. Inverse versus flat split moves the win rate by at most 1.6 points at any face rate. Inverse is kept because it makes the wildcard the event and Neighbours the common gift, not because it changes the outcome.

Finding — floor 5 is confirmed. At floor 6 with 10% faces the win rate is 81%. The planned 6 → 5 move is what keeps face tiles from making the daily a formality.

Model: the simulated player spends digit tiles before faces and the narrowest face first, sheds the most plentiful digit, and never sheds a face while holding a digit. It never misplaces a face — a real player will, so real win rates should sit slightly under these.
