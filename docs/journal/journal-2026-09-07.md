### The socket swatch is a well, measured rather than judged

SC 1.4.11 does not bind these swatches — the ruling already recorded on `T49`
stands and is not reopened here. What binds is the design's own bar: the socket
must read as cut, not laid on. `--surface-socket` at this size measures 1.025:1
against the felt, which is why `T44` draws the well from `--surface-raised`
inward instead.

Sampled from an element screenshot of the shipped swatch, three pixels wide down
the vertical centreline, `devicePixelRatio` 1, 19 rows for an 18px box (the box
starts at a fractional `y`, so the first and last rows are part outside):

```
y0  #071711   felt, outside the box
y1  #14271f   L .01699   band, darkest
y2  #112a21   L .01885   band
y3  #17392d   L .03298   band, fading
y4  #1a4234   L .04364
y5..y17 #1b4436  L .04634  the floor (--surface-raised)
y18 #2a5041   L .06611   the bottom rim (--rim-socket), part-clipped
```

- band against the floor: **1.438:1** at its darkest row, **1.399:1** at y2 —
  y2 is the row `T44`'s comment quotes, and it reproduces exactly.
- rim against the band: **1.686:1** — also exactly `T44`'s figure.
- rim against the floor: **1.205:1**, and that is a floor on the true value:
  y18 is only three-quarters inside the box, so the real rim is brighter.

The same element with the handover's `inset 0 1px 3px rgb(0 0 0 / 45%)` patched
in, sampled the same way in the same session: band `#1e3a2f`, L .0351, which is
**1.132:1** against the floor, under a rim at **1.107:1**. (`T44`'s comment says
1.161:1 for that variant; same order, a row's difference in where it was
sampled.)

So the deviation from the handover is justified by measurement rather than by
taste: the shipped value removes 63% of the floor's luminance where the band
lands, the handover's removes 24%. And the profile is what answers the question —
dark at the top, monotonic fade, brighter than the floor at the bottom. That
top-dark/bottom-light asymmetry is the light-from-above signature of a
depression; a chip laid on the felt paints a flat fill with neither end.
The well cue is present, graded and measurable. **It reads as cut.**

Honest limit: pixels prove the cue is painted and how far it separates from the
floor. Whether a 2px band at 1.44:1 is *noticed* at arm's length on a phone is a
perceptual question no sample settles. The claim here is narrower than the one
it replaces — not "one person's eye says it reads as a well", but "the well cue
exists, at these separations, and is three times the handover's".

## Step 3 — 320px, both locales, every phase

§8.5 forbids horizontal scroll at 320px. Swept `documentElement.scrollWidth`
against `clientWidth` from 320px to 680px in **4px steps** — between the tier
boundaries, not only at them — plus the maximum right edge over every element
with a non-zero box, in both locales, in every phase, under two conditions:

- **tall viewport (2400px), no vertical scrollbar** — `clientWidth === innerWidth`.
- **short viewport (420px), classic scrollbar taking layout width** —
  `clientWidth === innerWidth - 15`.

The second condition is not decoration. Headless Chromium shows no scrollbar
when the page fits vertically, and a sweep that only ever measures the first
condition cannot see issue #85 at all.

**Result, condition 1 (no scrollbar): zero overflow anywhere.** Title,
answering, feedback-correct, feedback-incorrect, overflow, game over; English
and Korean — twelve screen-locale pairs, each swept twice; `scrollWidth ===
clientWidth` at all 91 samples of every one.

**Result, condition 2 (classic scrollbar): the four game phases overflow, at
identical widths and by identical amounts, in both locales.**

| viewport | clientWidth | scrollWidth | overflow |
|---|---|---|---|
| 320 | 305 | 316 | 11px |
| 324 | 309 | 316 | 7px |
| 328 | 313 | 316 | 3px |
| 408 | 393 | 402 | 9px |
| 412 | 397 | 402 | 5px |
| 416 | 401 | 402 | 1px |

That is issue #85, reproduced to the pixel `spacing.css` already records
("viewport 408 gives clientWidth 393"). The widest element is `GameScreen`'s own
`.screen` in every case, sized by the rack beneath it. Two things this walk can
add:

- **#85 is not only the middle tier.** The narrow tier fails the same way at
  320px: the rack needs 292px plus 24px of arena padding = 316px, and a
  scrollbar leaves 305px. `spacing.css`'s "292px of the 296px a 320px viewport
  leaves" holds only without a scrollbar.
- **`M5.5e` did not make it worse.** The six overflow readings are *byte
  identical* across `answering`, `feedback` (both branches) and `overflow`, in
  both locales — and the overflow phase holds eleven tiles rather than ten. The
  rack is a fixed five-column grid, so the eleventh tile adds a row, not a
  column. Nothing this phase added ever became the widest element.

**The two screens this phase built are clean in both conditions.** Title:
nothing, at any width, either locale, scrollbar or not — the two-column rule
grid collapses to one column at 40rem and never sets the min-width. Game over:
nothing either. At 320px the English stat row wraps (`Longest streak` drops to
a second line, `top` 255 against 177) inside 296px of a 320px client; Korean
fits all three on one line in 199px. Both were the things flagged most likely to
overflow, and neither does.

## Step 4 — the traps, and which ones bit

Carried in from `T37`/`T42`, re-confirmed or newly hit:

- **`getComputedStyle` and the DOM are stale inside one `evaluate`.** Hit
  immediately: clicking tiles and then reading `Submit.disabled` in the same
  synchronous function reported `true` for a button React was about to enable.
  Every click and its consequence were split across calls after that.
- **The gallery stretches controls.** Not relied on: every figure above comes
  from `/`, reached by play. The gallery was not used at all.
- **`matches(':active')` is always false in an automated probe** — untested
  here; this phase declares no `:active` rule of its own.
- **Cropping to the border box misses an outset outline** — did not apply to the
  socket sample, whose shadow is `inset`, so the element screenshot is complete.
  Worth keeping for `M5.5g`'s focus fixtures.
- **New: an element that paints its own background is its own ground.** An
  ancestor-only background walk silently misreports a label on a button face as
  failing. Named above with the two elements it caught.
- **New: `clientWidth` is not `innerWidth`.** The whole of the #85 signature
  lives in the 15px between them, and it only appears when the page is tall
  enough to scroll. The viewport *height* is therefore a measurement decision,
  not a detail.

## Step 5 — what the read-only answer slots actually announce

Walked in the feedback phase, in both locales, off the accessibility tree.

English, after submitting `15` against `7 × 2`:

```
paragraph: "7 × 2 ="
generic:   "1"
generic:   "5"
status:    "Incorrect" / "Your answer: 15" / "Correct answer: 14"
```

Korean, after a correct `24` against `8 × 3`:

```
paragraph: "8 × 3 ="
generic:   "2"
generic:   "4"
status:    "정답" / "2 새 타일" / "4 새 타일" / "6 새 타일"
```

So a filled read-only slot contributes a bare digit with no role and no name, in
both locales. `Tile`'s roleless form drops `label` by design and the primitive
says so in a comment.

**The asymmetry `T46` reported is not reachable through play.** The empty
read-only socket — the one that carries its full name through `role="img"` —
requires a feedback phase with an unfilled slot, and `isSubmissionReady` gates
submission on `selectedTiles.length === getAnswerLength(equation)`. Every slot
is full at every feedback. The named branch exists for tests and fixtures; a
player never hears it. So the live question is only "two anonymous digits after
the equation, before the verdict".

Three answers were on the table. What the measurement says about each:

- **Fine as built.** In browse mode the reading order does the work: the digits
  sit between the equation and the verdict, in submission order, which is where
  a reader asking "what did I just build" would go. Nothing is wrong; it is only
  unlabelled.
- **The filled slot should carry its name, which means changing `Tile`.** This
  is the option the correct branch argues for. The *incorrect* status region
  says the submitted value out loud (`Your answer: 15`); the *correct* one does
  not — it says `Correct` and then lists the reward tiles. On a correct answer
  the slots are the only place the submitted answer appears at all.
- **`aria-hidden` the read-only group.** The same fact argues against it. Hiding
  it costs nothing on the incorrect branch and loses the submitted answer
  entirely on the correct one, for anyone listening to the live region rather
  than browsing.

Reported, not decided: this is a product call, and if it is taken it is a change
to `Tile`, `M5.5c`'s primitive, not a flow-screen edit.

One related observation, recorded because it bounds what the gate clause covers:
a correct answer taken at capacity 10 goes straight to `overflow`, which does
not mount the answer slots at all — the verdict is on screen without the tiles
it refers to until the discard is confirmed, after which the slots appear. That
is the neighbourhood of issue #93 (an overflowing correct answer skips the
feedback phase), already filed, and out of scope here.

## Step 6 — reduced motion

`M5.5e` adds no keyframes, so the check is that nothing it added animates
unconditionally.

Grep first, because a declaration that does not exist is a stronger result than
one that is neutralised: across the five stylesheets this phase touched
(`TitleScreen`, `GameOverScreen`, `FeedbackPanel`, `OverflowControls`,
`AnswerSlots`), **`transition` and `animation` appear zero times as properties**.
The only textual match is `GameOverScreen.module.css`'s comment recording that
the chop's stamp, hold and fade belong to `keyframes.css` and are deliberately
not declared here.

Confirmed in-page rather than left at a grep. At rest, every element this phase
added reads `transition-duration: 0s`, `animation-name: none`,
`animation-duration: 0s`: the chop, its reserved-height wrapper, the verdict,
`dd.primary`, the restart hint, the share status region.

Then the mechanism, by the `journal-2026-08-09` method — walk
`document.styleSheets`, flip `rule.media.mediaText` from
`(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` to `'all'`, re-read computed style, flip
back. No OS setting touched.

- Exactly **one** such media block reaches the document, from `global.css`.
- Flipped: every element above goes to `1e-05s` (the `0.01ms !important`), and
  `ActionButton`'s real transitions — `transform, box-shadow, background` at
  `0.1s, 0.1s, 0.15s` — collapse to `1e-05s` too.

So the neutraliser is live on these screens, and it has nothing of this phase's
to neutralise.
