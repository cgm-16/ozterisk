# 2026-09-12 — T62: the gate, measured — and three harness bugs that each read as a result

Every figure below came out of the page. Narrative is not evidence, and neither
is a harness you have not audited — three of the readings in this session were
wrong before they were right, and all three were wrong *silently*.

Measured against the dev server at `localhost:5173`, gallery only, in Chrome.

## The method, and the limit it hit

**#85 cannot be measured in a real top-level viewport from this harness.**
Stated plainly, because `T62` requires it to be:

- `resize_window` **reports success and does nothing.** Asked for `320×800`,
  then `500×800`; `innerWidth` stayed `954` through both. The tab is
  backgrounded (`document.visibilityState: "hidden"`), and a backgrounded tab
  does not take a window resize. Before a screenshot forced the window into a
  measurable state, `outerWidth` read `0` — the tell.
- `window.open('/gallery.html','…','width=320')` is **popup-blocked**, with and
  without a preceding synthetic click. A `computer` click's transient
  activation does not carry into the `javascript_tool` execution context.

So the readings below are iframe readings, and #85 **stays open**. `T62` says
closing it on an iframe is not acceptable and that remains true.

**But one thing narrowed the doubt, and it is worth recording.** The open
question `T56` left was whether an iframe's scrollbar behaves as a top-level
window's. On this machine the top-level window measured
`innerWidth 954 / clientWidth 939` — a **15px classic scrollbar taking layout
width** — and every iframe reading below also shows a 15px gutter. This Chrome
does not use overlay scrollbars, so the scrollbar condition #85 turns on is
present in both, and it is the same 15px in both. That does not make an iframe a
window. It does mean the specific mechanism #85 blames is reproduced rather than
absent, which is the failure mode a clean sweep on a zero-width overlay
scrollbar would have had.

## Three harness bugs, and why they belong in the record

Every one of them produced a plausible reading. None of them threw.

1. **I swept `main *`, and the gallery renders into `div.stage`.** `main` belongs
   to the *game screens*; the four `interaction` boards render no `main` at all.
   So my first 320px sweep measured **zero elements** on hover, focus-visible,
   disabled and reduced-motion — and reported them clean, in both locales. A
   sweep over nothing is indistinguishable from a sweep that found nothing. The
   fix was an element count per state, which is now in the table above and is
   the only reason the table can be trusted.

2. **A media-query flip needs time to settle, and reading too early returns the
   previous direction.** At one point `overflow-marked` read `1e-05s` in the
   *normal* direction — which looks exactly like "this state has no motion", the
   false negative the task file warns about. Raising the settle from 110ms to
   450ms made it read `oz-rim-reject 0.13s`. It reproduced cleanly once, which
   is how it was caught.

3. **A stylesheet rule object dies with its document.** After reloading the
   iframe for the Korean pass, my cached reference to the reduced-motion rule
   pointed at a detached stylesheet: flipping it changed nothing, and all three
   reads came back identical with full durations — which looks exactly like
   "reduced motion is broken." Re-acquiring the rule from the live document
   fixed it.

The general lesson is the one this milestone keeps relearning in new clothes.
`journal-2026-08-12.md` says narrative is not evidence. This adds: **a
measurement is not evidence either, until the instrument has been checked
against a case it must fail.** Bug 1 was found by counting what was measured,
bug 2 by a reading that contradicted a known-good one, bug 3 by a result too
uniform to be true. None would have been found by looking at the numbers and
nodding.

## The verdict

The `M5.5g` gate, as `T57` wrote it, in five parts:

| Clause | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Gallery covers hover, focus-visible, disabled, reduced-motion; every state renders what its name claims | **met** |
| §8.5 walked with measured evidence at 320px **in a real top-level viewport** | **missed** (#85) — iframe only; the harness cannot foreground a window |
| Reduced motion read **per moment**, both directions | **missed in part** (#114) — 12 of 15 moments; `8c`, `9b`, `10e` have no resting surface |
| No rack badge overlaps the engraved digit, either locale, either face | **met** |
| `docs/design-system/` holds only what the product still reads | **met** |

**Met in three parts of five. The gate stays as written.** That is the third
time this milestone — `M5.5b` missed one of four, `M5.5f` missed its inventory
clause, and both left the standard alone. A gate is the bar, not a description
of what got done, and moving it to fit the result is the one edit that would
make every earlier phase's honesty worthless.

Worth naming: **the clause I missed is one I wrote.** `T57` tightened the
roadmap row to require a real top-level viewport, five days after `T56`
discovered the harness cannot produce one. Writing a bar this environment cannot
clear was not wrong — the bar is right, and #85 deserves it — but it should be
recorded as a deliberate choice rather than discovered as a surprise.

Two issues carry the misses, and each is named in the row it belongs to: #114
for the three moments with no resting surface, and #85, which stays open with
the gutter-equality reading attached.
