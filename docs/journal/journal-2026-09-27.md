# 2026-09-27 — Rack steps: a device reading, an experiment, and a pick

## #159: the 15 and 10 thresholds were made for five columns

Ori played Classic on an **iPhone 13 mini** (iOS Safari, 375px CSS width, a rack
container of about 351px, so the wide 7-column small size). The step from 15 to
10 felt awkward: a whole row sits empty before the rack drops to ten. The code
explains it. 15 and 10 are whole rows only at five columns, but the mid size was
6 × 3, a fixed 18 cells. So at 12 and 11 its third row was all plugs.

## The experiment (#177, closed unmerged)

A throwaway branch switched schemes by `?rack=`. `rack-run.mjs`, a headless
Chromium driver kept in the session scratchpad and not the repo, played real
Classic runs at 320, 375, 402 and 430px, and read the rack after every
submission. Scheme A reproduced the predicted all-plug rows before anything
changed, so the instrument could fail.

| Scheme | All-plug rows | Steps (submission) |
|---|---|---|
| A, today | 12, 11 at every width; also 18–16 at 320 | 10, 20 |
| B, 5 × 3 mid | none from 375 up; 18–16 at 320 | 10, 20 |
| C, whole rows only | none | 10, 16, 20, plus 4 at 320 |

**Ori picked B.** C's rack shrinks at 12, then grows at 10. The 320px row is
accepted, since those phones are rare. T82 builds it, and `M6·2` retires with it:
no size below twenty now starts with a plug.

## Also measured, and not taken

- **Overflow in the prompt.** Seating the tile past capacity inside the discard
  prompt, instead of on the rail, took the overflow screen from +87px over
  feedback to +8px in Endless, and from +78px to −1px in Classic (375 × 629).
  Ori judged it not needed yet. Nobody judged on a device how its longer drop,
  from the prompt into the freed seat, reads. If it is revisited, start there.
- **The screen scrolls on small phones in every phase.** On production, 375px
  wide, page heights are: title 756, answering 578–591, feedback 709–741,
  overflow 796–800. An iPhone SE (667px screen, about 548 visible with Safari's
  bars) scrolls even while answering. That's a separate job from the rack, and
  no issue is filed yet.
- **Ori's first idea for the overflow tile, seating it on a sealed plug,** was
  never built. It has no plug to sit on in Endless, at the first round of 15
  and 10, or for a second excess tile with one plug. That would take a rail
  fallback, which means two looks for one moment.

## Discard scroll (#180, #182 → T83)

- **It isn't just small phones.** On production, the page is 776–853px tall at a
  discard at every common width (360, 393, 412, 430), and answering reaches
  790–845px late in a run. Page height doesn't shrink as the width grows, so
  whether it scrolls depends only on how much of the page is visible. That makes
  most iPhones in Safari, and 360px Androids, scroll at every discard.
- **The prompt, the rail and the rack together are at most 379px tall** (Classic,
  320px wide, narrow 6 × 4 rack). They fit even the SE's 548px. So one scroll can
  show all three, but about 450px sits above them, so no layout tweak gets the
  page under 548.
- **Experiment #182 readings.** Scroll distance and where the equation ends up:

  | Viewport | Scrolled | Equation |
  |---|---|---|
  | 375 × 548 | 228px | leaves the top |
  | 393 × 660 | 116px | stays at 104 |
  | 430 × 740 | 36px | stays at 184 |

  At 393 × 660, production had the rack's bottom at 776 with no scroll; the
  experiment brings it to the fold. Ori played it on a phone and judged it
  "much better".
- **Not measured:** where the next round starts. The page stays where the
  discard scrolled it, and the next equation may begin partly off the top.
  Worth watching for in playtests.
- **Real-scrollbar reading for #130.** Headed Chromium with macOS scroll bars
  always shown, at a 408px viewport, gave a 15px gutter and a 393px content
  width. That puts it in the middle tier (66px tiles), and nothing overflows
  in any phase, in en or ko.

## Face rate ramp (T84)

**Method.** The simulator plays Classic on the real `gameReducer`,
`generateKindEquation`, `createInitialInventory` and selectors. Only the reward
draw is re-implemented, with the same face table, so the rate can follow a
schedule. Each schedule played 3,000 runs on the same seeds. The bot:
- answers correctly with probability "skill" whenever its hand can build the
  answer, spending exact digits before the narrowest face;
- on a miss, spends its most redundant digits;
- discards the digits it holds the most copies of, and faces last.

The script was a scratchpad file (`face-sim.ts`, run with `npx vite-node`) and
is not committed.

**Instrument check.** A rate of 0 earns no faces; faces per run scale with the
rate; and at a flat 8% it earns about 4, which is what Ori saw in play. The bot
plays below the M7 handoff's simulator: 33% wins without faces, against 47%.
So compare schedules with each other; the rates don't forecast real play.

| Schedule | Win, skill 95% | Win, skill 85% | Faces earned / run | Earned at ≤ 10 sockets |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| flat 0.00 | 33.0% | 23.8% | 0.0 | 0.0 |
| flat 0.05 | 44.3% | 34.8% | 2.4 | 0.4 |
| flat 0.08 (shipped before T84) | 52.0% | 41.8% | 4.1 | 0.8 |
| flat 0.10 | 57.2% | 46.3% | 5.4 | 1.1 |
| flat 0.16 | 70.8% | 60.3% | 9.5 | 2.3 |
| ramp 0.08 → 0.16 | 60.7% | 49.4% | 6.2 | 1.8 |
| ramp 0.04 → 0.16 | 55.8% | 44.8% | 4.7 | 1.6 |
| ramp 0.08 → 0.24 | 68.7% | 59.5% | 8.7 | 3.1 |
| ramp 0.05 → 0.20 | 62.2% | 50.0% | 6.2 | 2.2 |
| ramp 0.02 → 0.20 | 59.0% | 47.4% | 5.1 | 2.1 |
| 0.08, 0.16 from capacity 10 | 56.5% | 46.3% | 5.1 | 1.8 |
| 0.08 → 0.16 by capacity | 60.0% | 48.3% | 6.0 | 1.7 |

In the table the ramps end at submission 28. As built, the ramp ends on the
last submission, 29. Rerun that way, 0.05 → 0.20 gives 61.2% / 49.4%, 6.1
faces, 2.2 late.

**Why late faces stay scarce even when the rate doubles.** The last third of a
run is about 10 submissions, and fewer of them are correct, so there are only
about 15 reward tiles to draw from. Even a 0.08 → 0.24 ramp earns only about
three faces there.

**Not measured:** how real players spend faces. A person may hoard faces or
misplace them, which the bot doesn't do.
