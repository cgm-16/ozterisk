# 2026-10-26 — Puzzle through a window

## What Ori found

Ori played Puzzle on #205's preview and found the loop dull: look at the
table for a while, then solve everything or fail. All eight rows are face up,
so the one big decision comes first and the rest is typing it in.

- One tester has also played the baseline. Ori, not the tester, found it too
  boring. That is a designer note, not a playtest result. Since a tester has
  played, §6.1 of the M8 design spec stays as written: changing it now would
  move the gate.
- Ori's "the next round is almost always a killscreen" meant the next
  *table*. The playtest set deals Medium, then Hard.

## The measurement

**Instrument.** The research solver (`03-puzzle/sims/solver.ts`) plus an exact
(score, end-row) count over every line.

- **Tables:** the three playtest tables, and 300 live in-band deals on each of
  seeds 1 and 2.
- **Code:** `feat/M8-puzzle-prototype` at 3ccd37b.
- **Calibration:** reproduced the sample: par 7, and 38 of 16,893 lines at
  par. The shipped `dealPuzzle` dealt the same 600 tables as the research
  plant-plus-band.
- **Caveat:** line shares count every line once, so they describe the tables,
  not how players play.
- **Location:** the scripts and logs are in
  `docs/research/game-modes/03-puzzle/sims/` on `feat/M10-puzzle-window`.

Findings:

- **By the lookahead bot, the set is Medium, Hard, Hard.** On the sample too,
  the lookahead bot scores 6 against par 7. Live deals are 85.7% Medium
  (82.7% on seed 2). So the set is harsher than live play: after table 1,
  both remaining tables are the hard kind.
- **A mistake costs most early in the table.** On live deals, a wrong discard
  costs 2.95, 2.40, 1.98, 1.66, 1.45, 1.24 and 1.00 rows on rows 1–7. It
  leaves no line that reaches row 8 in 38%, 31%, 25%, 14%, 5%, 0% and 0% of
  cases. With par at 7 of 8, an early slip lands near 4 or 5.
- **The end rule does not cause it.** The alternative rule was: a row the hand
  can't attempt scores 0, and play goes on.
  - It leaves par unchanged on all three tables and all 600 live deals, and
    saves only 0.05–0.15 rows per mistake.
  - Under the shipped rule, about 80% of off-par live lines already play all
    eight rows.
  - A mistake costs a lot because the hand runs short of tiles, not because
    the run ends.

### Two corrections to what Claude told Ori in session

- Claude said the harshness was not a test artifact, because live deals pass
  the same filter. Live deals do pass it, but they are mostly Medium. The set
  is harsher.
- Claude said the zero-and-play-on rule would turn "solve or fail" into a
  gradient. It does not, as measured above. Ori first chose it, then reversed
  the choice on this evidence.

## Decisions

- A baseline pass does not merge the face-up build: the kill test is a floor,
  not an obligation to ship. Its result still picks Batch 2 (journal
  2026-10-19).
- M10 is cancelled only if most baseline testers never look at the ledger.

### M10 — Puzzle Window (#207)

- **What the player sees.**
  - A fixed eight-row table, seen through a window of *k* rows that includes
    the current one. One more row appears after each submission.
  - Rows beyond the window show face down.
  - A row entering the window appears instantly: no new motion.
- ***k*.** A new dial, `PUZZLE_WINDOW`, starting at 3. A simulation sets it:
  the smallest *k* where reading the window captures most of what seeing the
  whole table is worth.
- **Par.** The score of a bot that reads the same window, shown up front.
  - At each step the bot takes the move with the best line inside the window.
    It ranks lines by correct answers, then tiles in hand at the window's end,
    then distinct digits, then a fixed order on the move.
  - A clairvoyant par would be a promise a fair player can't keep.
- **Result.** A score at or above par gets the gold Made par, with the margin
  in the stats.
- **Deal filter.** M8's three rules, measured on the window bot:
  - the window par is at or above a floor;
  - the myopic bot scores below it;
  - the window bot's line has enough only-moves.

  They are new dials, with floors that keep killscreen tables out of live
  deals. M8's band dials never reached `main`, and M10 drops them.
- **Unchanged.** Eight rows (`PUZZLE_ROWS`), capacity five, and the end rule.
- **Copy.** The name stays Puzzle / 묘수풀이 for the prototype. The hint and
  How-to-play are rewritten for the window. Whether 묘수풀이 still fits is
  decided after M10's playtest.

## Settled after the window sim

The window sim is in `docs/research/game-modes/03-puzzle/sims/window/` on
`feat/M10-puzzle-window`. It ran against 3ccd37b, and its numbers are bot
scores, not player data. Its findings are in the M10 design spec §5.5.

- **Three rows, not four.** Claude read "most" as a captured share of 0.75.
  *k* = 3 misses that bar on unfiltered plants (0.742 pooled), and *k* = 4
  clears it. Ori chose 3 anyway, for three reasons:
  - On the tables the filter deals, the window bot's par sits 0.18 rows
    below clairvoyant play at 3, and 0.09 at 4.
  - A smaller window breaks up the up-front stare more.
  - At 3 a revealed row costs even careful reading a point on about 1 dealt
    table in 6, against 1 in 10 at 4, so reveals keep some bite.

  The floors stay at window par 6 and 2 window only-moves.
- **The preference confound.** A side review found that M10's preference
  question favoured M10. Baseline testers met face-up on a fixed Medium,
  Hard, Hard set and played it first, while M10 deals only filtered tables.
  Ori's mitigation is to ask against each tester's fourth face-up table,
  which M8's protocol deals live. The order effect stays as a known limit.
